Semiconductor financials have two features: high volatility and many accounting conventions. Consumer-stock frameworks tend to produce wrong conclusions here.

## Start with four numbers

1. **Gross margin**: pricing power and manufacturing cost. Fabless 40%–60%, foundry 30%–55%, OSAT 15%–30%, equipment 40%–55%.
2. **Inventory days**: inventory divided by daily cost of goods sold. Falling inventory days often precede a price recovery.
3. **Capex-to-sales**: 20%–35% for manufacturing and memory, much lower for equipment and design.
4. **Free cash flow**: in capex-heavy stages, profit does not equal cash.

## Why valuation is hard

Semiconductors are cyclical. P/E looks low at the top (high earnings) and high or negative at the bottom. Using P/E alone systematically buys the top.

Better comparisons:

- **P/B**: for asset-heavy manufacturing and memory with volatile earnings.
- **P/S**: for high-growth designers without profits.
- **EV/EBITDA**: across different capital structures.
- **Normalised earnings**: restore mid-cycle margins and utilisation.

## Metrics by stage

- Design: design wins, customer concentration, ASP, inventory turns.
- Foundry: utilisation, wafer ASP, node mix, depreciation/revenue.
- Memory: bit shipments, contract price, inventory weeks.
- Equipment: book-to-bill, backlog, service revenue share.
- OSAT: utilisation, advanced-packaging mix, test hours.

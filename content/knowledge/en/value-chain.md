Semiconductor supply chains are usually drawn as a straight line. What actually determines profit is **capital intensity** and **bargaining power** at each stage. The same chip passes through eight stages, and each one keeps a very different share of the value.

## One chain, two kinds of business

The main flow runs EDA/IP → design → wafer fabrication → packaging and test → modules and systems. Equipment and materials sit outside that flow but enable every manufacturing step: they are a **support layer**.

- **EDA/IP**: software licences and royalties, gross margins above 80%, very high switching costs.
- **Fabless design**: 40%–60% gross margin, 15%–25% R&D intensity; product definition and design wins decide the outcome.
- **Foundry/IDM**: 30%–55% gross margin, but a leading-edge fab can cost over USD 10bn and depreciation absorbs much of the profit.
- **Memory**: the most standardised product; gross margin swings from negative to 60%.
- **Equipment**: 40%–55% gross margin, revenue follows fab capex and swings harder than end demand.
- **Materials**: 20%–40% gross margin, long qualification cycles, smoother revenue.
- **Packaging and test**: 15%–25% for traditional packaging; advanced packaging lifts it materially.
- **Modules**: 5%–15% gross margin, driven by inventory turns and procurement timing.

## The smile curve is incomplete

The “high at both ends, low in the middle” story is only half true. Manufacturing is capital-intensive but commands strong pricing power when capacity is scarce. Equipment and materials earn excess returns from qualification barriers. What persists is **certification lock-in**, not position on a diagram.

## How to use this desk

The value-chain page shows company count, market cap and median 30-day return by stage. Three uses:

1. **Structure**: which stage holds the most market cap and the most companies.
2. **Dispersion**: inside one stage, leaders and laggards often differ more than stages do.
3. **Transmission**: equipment activity usually leads fab revenue by two to four quarters; memory pricing leads module inventory.

## Common misreadings

- Treating a localisation rate as verified volume production. Building a tool and supplying it reliably are different things.
- Applying stage gross margin to a single company. Product mix can move margins by 20 points inside one stage.
- Ignoring FX. This desk converts market cap to USD, but company accounts remain in local currency.

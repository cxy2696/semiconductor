Semiconductors have never been a pure market. Export controls, subsidies and industrial policy can change a company's customer base and capacity footprint overnight.

## Three policy tools

- **Export controls**: restrictions on tools, EDA, AI chips and technology, usually enforced through lists and licences.
- **Subsidies and tax credits**: the US CHIPS Act, the EU Chips Act, and programmes in Japan and Korea.
- **Localisation targets**: domestic-content and local-capacity goals that redirect supply chains.

## Transmission channels

1. **Equipment vendors**: for companies with high China revenue, controls change order visibility directly.
2. **Fabs**: restricted access to leading-edge tools changes expansion timing and node roadmaps.
3. **Designers**: limits on EDA and IP affect advanced chip development.
4. **Materials**: controls and localisation targets create both opportunity and competition.

## How to research policy

- Track **official texts**, not headlines. Lists, licence scope and effective dates determine impact.
- Separate **short-term shocks** from **structural change**.
- Do not extrapolate linearly. Policies are revised and enforcement varies.

## Common misreadings

- Treating “import substitution” as guaranteed revenue. Substitution needs qualification and volume ramp.
- Treating subsidies as profit. They usually come with capex and local-employment conditions.
- Ignoring third countries. Transshipment, overseas fabs and alliances change real-world effects.

Equipment and materials companies do not sell chips. They determine who can build chips and at what cost.

## Equipment: a lever on capex

The global equipment market is roughly USD 100bn-plus, split into front-end and back-end:

- **Front-end**: lithography, etch, deposition, CMP, cleaning, ion implantation, furnaces, metrology and inspection.
- **Back-end**: dicing, die attach, bonding, molding, testers, handlers and probe cards.

Revenue comes from fab capex, so it swings harder than end demand. The industry watches **book-to-bill**: above 1 means orders are outpacing deliveries. A high service and spares mix dampens cyclicality.

## Materials: a function of wafer output

Materials follow wafer output and are smoother than equipment:

- **Wafers**: 300mm and 200mm, highly concentrated.
- **Photoresist**: EUV resist is one of the hardest materials problems.
- **Specialty gases**: NF₃, WF₆, SiH₄ and others, often above 6N purity.
- **Targets and slurries**: key consumables for PVD and CMP.
- **Masks, substrates, molding compounds**: tied to lithography, packaging and reliability.

## Concentration is pricing power

Both equipment and materials are concentrated. One supplier for lithography, one for EUV mask inspection. That supports pricing power but also geopolitical risk: export controls can rewrite a customer base quickly.

## The real state of localisation

Mainland China has scaled domestic suppliers in mature-node etch, cleaning, CMP and furnaces; lithography and high-end metrology remain gaps. Localisation should be judged by **volume production and repeat orders**, not by the existence of a product.

## What to watch

- Equipment: book-to-bill, backlog, China revenue exposure, service mix.
- Materials: capacity additions, long-term contract prices, localisation rate, raw-material costs.
- Both: customer concentration and changes to export-control lists.

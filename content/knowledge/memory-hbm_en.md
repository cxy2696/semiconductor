Memory behaves like a commodity: standardised products, transparent pricing, and prices that can double or halve within a year when supply and demand diverge.

## Three product families

- **DRAM**: dynamic random-access memory storing charge in capacitors and requiring refresh; used in modules and HBM.
- **NAND**: non-volatile memory read by page and erased by block; used in SSDs and mobile storage.
- **NOR**: small capacity, high reliability; used for code storage and automotive electronics.

## Three variables in the cycle

1. **Bit supply**: set by capex and node migration; new capacity takes 12–18 months.
2. **Bit demand**: servers, phones, PCs and AI.
3. **Inventory**: weeks of inventory at makers, module houses and the channel determine price elasticity.

When supply grows faster than demand, prices fall and makers cut output. When inventory bottoms and demand recovers, prices rise and capex re-accelerates. The loop runs roughly three to five years.

## What HBM changes

HBM stacks DRAM dies vertically with TSVs and connects to a GPU over an ultra-wide interface, reaching multi-TB/s bandwidth. Three structural effects:

- **Capacity intensity**: the same number of bits consumes far more wafers and capacity than standard DRAM.
- **Yield barrier**: more stacked layers mean harder yield and thermal challenges.
- **Customer lock-in**: HBM is qualified jointly with GPU vendors, stabilising supply relationships.

This is why HBM can partly decouple from the plain DRAM price cycle.

## What to watch

- Bit shipment growth and contract prices.
- Inventory weeks at makers and in the channel.
- Capex and wafer capacity.
- HBM stack height, yield and customer qualification.

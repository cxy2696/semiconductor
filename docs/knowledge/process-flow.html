Wafer fabrication: eight process families

From cleaning and lithography to etch and metrology, see how a wafer becomes a chip and why step count raises equipment and materials intensity.

Fabrication is not a single printing step. The same pattern-transfer loop repeats hundreds of times. A leading-edge logic chip can exceed one thousand process steps and take three to four months to build.

The eight process families

  1. Cleaning: removes particles, metal ions and organic residue before and after critical steps.
  2. Oxidation/diffusion: high-temperature furnaces grow oxides and drive dopants in.
  3. Lithography: coat, expose and develop to transfer the mask pattern into resist.
  4. Etch: plasma selectively removes material to transfer the pattern into a film.
  5. Deposition: CVD, PECVD, PVD and ALD grow insulating, conductive and barrier films.
  6. Ion implantation: sets dopant type and concentration for source/drain and wells.
  7. CMP: slurry and a pad flatten the surface for multi-layer interconnect.
  8. Metrology and inspection: measure thickness, critical dimension and overlay, and find defects.

Why step count matters

More steps mean more equipment, more materials consumed and harder yield control at the same time. That is why a leading-edge wafer is worth far more than a mature-node wafer, and why equipment and materials capture a larger share of that value.

Cleanrooms and yield

Critical areas of a leading-edge fab reach ISO Class 1: no more than one particle above 0.5µm per cubic foot. Yield is not the result of one step; it is the product of defect densities across all steps. A single contaminated chamber can scrap an entire lot.

Back end and advanced packaging

After front-end completion, wafers move to dicing, attach, bonding, molding, balling and final test. Traditional packaging is labour- and material-intensive. Advanced packaging increasingly looks like front-end work: lithography for redistribution layers, deposition and CMP for bump and bond interfaces, cleanrooms throughout.

What to watch

  • Utilisation: above 90% usually signals pricing power and expansion.
  • Wafer ASP: node mix changes the average price directly.
  • Yield ramp: the core test of a new fab.
  • Depreciation as a share of revenue: higher at leading edge.

The silicon cycle: locating where we are

Capex, inventory and end demand drive the semiconductor cycle. Instead of forecasting the turn, use a small set of verifiable indicators to locate the present.

Semiconductors are cyclical, but “the cycle” is not a slogan. It is a set of observable variables: capex, inventory, pricing and utilisation.

Four phases

  1. Shortage: demand beats expectations, utilisation approaches 100%, prices rise.
  2. Expansion: capex accelerates, equipment orders lead, sentiment turns optimistic.
  3. Glut: new capacity arrives, inventory builds, prices soften.
  4. Clearing: prices fall, capex is cut, marginal capacity exits.

Equities usually lead fundamentals: equipment orders lead fab revenue by two to four quarters; memory prices lead maker profits by one to two.

Five indicators

  • Capex: industry capex growth leads equipment revenue.
  • Utilisation: above 90% or below 70% are both signals.
  • Inventory days: inflection at makers and in the channel.
  • Contract versus spot prices: price elasticity in memory and some analog parts.
  • Book-to-bill: orders relative to deliveries.

How this desk uses it

The market state on the home page combines breadth, median 30-day return and dispersion. It does not forecast direction; it describes whether the market is broadly rising, diverging or broadly falling.

  • High breadth and positive median: expansion.
  • Low breadth and negative median: contraction.
  • Otherwise: neutral.

Together with leading and lagging stages, it shows how capital rotates inside the chain.

Common misreadings

  • Reading a share-price rally as an upcycle. Prices can front-run fundamentals or simply follow liquidity.
  • Extrapolating one quarter into a year. Quarterly swings are enormous.
  • Ignoring FX and inventory accounting effects on profit.

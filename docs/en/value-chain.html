The value chain

Semiconductors are not a straight line but a network of a main flow and support layers. Understanding how each stage earns money and which metrics bind it matters more than memorising company names.

The tooling layer of chip design: EDA software, instruction-set and processor IP, interface IP and design services. Highest gross margins and recurring revenue, but limited scale and deeply ecosystem-locked.

How it earns money

Revenue is license plus royalty based. Switching costs are high, which supports pricing power. Watch license-versus-royalty mix, royalty per chip, and IP certification at leading-edge nodes.

Key metrics

  • Royalty rate
  • Renewal rate
  • R&D intensity

Median 30D
-15.51%

Fabless companies define products and complete front-end and back-end design, outsourcing fabrication to foundries. Outcomes hinge on product definition, design-win velocity and access to foundry capacity.

How it earns money

A light-asset model with high R&D intensity (15%–25% of revenue) and cash tied up in inventory and capacity prepayments. Watch design wins, customer concentration, ASP, gross margin and inventory turns.

Key metrics

  • Design wins
  • Customer concentration
  • Inventory turns

$3.12T
Median 30D
-20.62%

The most capital-intensive layer: transferring design patterns onto silicon. Foundries earn on utilisation and node mix; IDMs carry both design and manufacturing. Leading edge is a handful of players, mature nodes are far more competitive.

How it earns money

A single leading-edge fab can cost north of USD 10bn, so depreciation is heavy. Watch utilisation, wafer ASP, node mix, capex intensity and depreciation as a share of revenue.

Key metrics

  • Utilisation
  • Wafer ASP
  • Depreciation / revenue

$2.45T
Median 30D
-31.91%

The most standardised semiconductor category: DRAM, NAND, NOR and HBM. Deeply cyclical and oligopolistic; pricing is set by supply, demand and inventory. HBM has moved memory back to the centre of the AI story.

How it earns money

Track bit supply/demand and the cost-per-bit curve: bit shipment growth, contract pricing, inventory weeks and capex. For HBM, watch stack height, yield and customer qualification.

Key metrics

  • Bit shipment growth
  • Contract price
  • Inventory weeks

The picks-and-shovels layer: lithography, etch, deposition, CMP, cleaning, ion implantation, metrology, inspection and test. Long technical and qualification cycles make equipment a leading indicator for fab revenue.

How it earns money

Revenue follows fab capex, so it swings harder than end demand. Watch book-to-bill, backlog, China revenue exposure and the share of service and spares (recurring revenue).

Key metrics

  • Book-to-bill
  • Backlog
  • Service revenue share

Silicon wafers, photoresist, specialty gases, sputtering targets, CMP slurries, substrates and molding compounds. Wafer and photoresist supply is concentrated, qualification is slow, and revenue is smoother than equipment.

How it earns money

Pricing scales with purity and specification, and customer stickiness is high. Watch capacity additions, long-term contract prices, localisation rates and raw-material costs such as polysilicon, silver and copper.

Key metrics

  • Capacity additions
  • Contract price
  • Localisation rate

$128.2B

-27.07%

Turns bare die into shippable parts: dicing, attach, bonding, molding, balling and final test. Advanced packaging (2.5D/3D, CoWoS, hybrid bonding) is now an AI compute bottleneck and captures rising value.

How it earns money

Traditional OSAT margins are modest (15%–25%); advanced packaging lifts them materially. Watch utilisation, advanced-packaging revenue mix, CoWoS/HBM-linked orders and test hours.

Key metrics

  • Utilisation
  • Advanced packaging mix
  • Test hours

2

$24.4B
Median 30D
-38.36%

Memory modules, boards and subsystems. Close to end demand, fast-turning but thin-margin, and a sensitive window into channel inventory and end-market health.

How it earns money

The core is inventory timing: cheap procurement amplifies profit in upcycles and losses in downcycles. Watch inventory days, contract-versus-spot price spread and channel inventory.

Key metrics

  • Inventory days
  • Contract-spot spread
  • Channel inventory
